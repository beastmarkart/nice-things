This whole project started because someone told me I had to watch *Project Hail Mary* because I like sci-fi, and it ended up being fucking dumb. The premise, the science, and the Petrova Band thing were cool, but it got totally ruined by a dancing rock and this idiot acting like the Deadpool guy—or whatever actor it is who isn't the Deadpool guy, but is basically just as dumb as the Deadpool guy. It proved my exact point: this is why we can't have good things. People are fucking morons, so studios dumb everything down for streaming, and that's why there are barely any good movies or shows.

Instead of just bitching about it, I decided to put my money where my mouth is, actually watch a bunch of hard sci-fi, cyberpunk, and adjacent movies, and see where they actually fall.

## // EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

* **Tight logic and closed systems:** Establish rules and stick to them. The narrative needs to operate within a coherent, smart system. No jumping the shark at the end by inventing magic solutions or injecting sudden sentimentality to resolve complex theoretical problems.
* **No sellout tropes:** Zero tolerance for mainstream pandering. Cut the annoying quippy dialogue, the generic military guy introduced in act one, forced romantic subplots that dilute the central concept, and legacy cameos tossed in just to pander to casual viewers.
* **Aesthetics, mood, and atmosphere:** The visuals, cinematography, and sound design must carry real informational and sensory weight. The environment has to feel tangible, deliberate, and immersive rather than serving as generic digital background filler.
* **Execution over fake complexity:** Pacing and clarity matter. A movie can be minimalist or low-budget and still succeed if the concept is handled cleanly without hand-holding. Conversely, intentionally confusing the audience is not depth, and sterile, impenetrable narrative convolution is not good storytelling.
